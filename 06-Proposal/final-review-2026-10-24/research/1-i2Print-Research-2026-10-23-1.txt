Assessment
i2Print's most defensible opportunity is to become an easier-to-buy-from, dependable
NYC production partner for repeat business customers. A price-led generic storefront puts
it into direct comparison with national platforms. A stronger site should explain the work it can
handle, prove delivery capability and make complex inquiries easier to complete. This is a
strategic recommendation, not a measured forecast.
The strongest independent finding is documented institutional business: NYCEDC lists i2Print in
FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 procurement reports. The latest report records $11,238.46 spent
with the matching business/address in FY2026. A 2018 Long Island City Partnership report also
credits i2Print as printer. These support a local institutional footprint, not a claim of market
leadership. [S6-S9]
The most consequential technology finding is that the public FAQ already describes automatic
standard-product pricing, proof approval and tracking. OnPrintShop's current product family
includes AI image tools and preflight improvements. Audit what i2Print actually licenses and uses
before estimating replacement work. Software capability is not evidence of customer adoption.
[S3, S18]
Ownership remains unverified. Thomas Leong is associated with the company and his public
profile is indexed as Manager; that does not establish legal ownership, sole ownership or a
founder role. The founding date is inconsistent: the official site says 2000 and LinkedIn lists 1990.
[S1, S4, S5]

Practical outlook: growth is available by winning a better mix of jobs, increasing retention and
reducing handling costs. It is not safe to budget on a broad boom in commodity print. Owning an
offset press is not obsolete; match the process to run length, quality, finishing and total job cost.

5. What is trending, and what deserves caution
Stronger opportunity themes: shorter and more frequent digital runs; packaging/labels and
versioned products; integrated ordering and approval; fast, clear customer communication;
reusable customer specifications; and workflow automation. Smithers identifies digital production
and packaging as growth areas. FESPA's 2025 study, reported in May 2026, covered 774
businesses in 89 countries and found nearly half without automation and around 40% without AI.
Adoption is uneven, leaving scope for practical improvements. These are international findings,
not i2Print's status. [S19, S20, S24]

7. AI: what is present versus what is possible
Confirmed public functionality: the FAQ describes standard online price calculation,
custom-quote contact, tracking, saved designs and PDF proof approval. These are described
workflows, not evidence of AI or a transaction-tested audit. [S3]
Vendor capability: OnPrintShop v13.0 release notes describe AI image editing, upload preflight

unknown. No public evidence reviewed confirms i2Print uses a specific LLM, AI estimator, local
model, autonomous marketing agent or production agent. Do not describe them as AI-free or
AI-powered based on a template storefront. [S18]

integration on October 20, 2026 covering catalog discovery, configuration, design previews and

Start with structured inquiry intake, after checking existing features and APIs. It is narrower
than replacing checkout and builds the clean data needed for quoting. Choose a small staff-only
pilot, evaluate 20-30 representative inquiries, compare extraction to human review, and keep
customer sends manual initially. This sample size is a proposed feasibility test, not an assurance
of production reliability.
Self-hosted software does not automatically mean local AI. Map where prompts, artwork and
customer information travel. Prefer minimizing sensitive content, access by role, documented
retention and reviewed outputs. A local model needs actual hardware and accuracy testing; do
not buy a GPU merely to satisfy the word AI. No model or infrastructure purchase is
recommended without that assessment.

9. Growth tests, economics and next 90 days
Days 1-15: confirm company history/ownership, product capabilities, current software version,
server boundary and high-value customer types. Review an anonymized order sample for
product, account, job cost, revisions, turnaround and repeat frequency. Baseline quote speed, win
rate, reprints and handling time. Use existing records, not invented metrics.
Days 16-45: develop the website in the dedicated project once code arrives, create two
audience/offer drafts and collect approved work examples. Test clearer inquiry intake with staff.
Review current OnPrintShop features before committing to any replacement.
Days 46-90: launch only when site and delivery checks pass; this is compatible with the earlier
6-10 week planning window, not a new deadline. Run the two audience tests and a small
inquiry-assistant pilot if separately authorized. Compare actual effort, quote conversion and
contribution margin with baseline, then choose the next automation phase.
Illustrative economics, not an i2Print forecast: 50 inquiries/month x 10 minutes saved = 8.3
hours/month. At an assumed $30/hour internal labor value, that is about $250/month of capacity
value, not necessarily payroll savings. An assumed $2,000 implementation and $50/month
running cost would have a simple payback near 10 months if all savings are realized and
integration/maintenance costs are included in those assumptions. At only 20 inquiries/month, the
same assumptions give about $100/month of capacity value and roughly 40-month payback after
$50 running cost. Actual volumes and costs could change the decision entirely.
For growth, calculate incremental contribution from acquired accounts after print costs, finishing,
shipping subsidies, rework, service labor and acquisition costs. Higher sales with worse job
margins are not a successful automation result. An ambitious sales target needs baseline
revenue, capacity and retention data that are not available here.
10. Open questions that materially change the plan
• Who legally owns i2Print, who founded it, and how do 1990 and 2000 relate? No verified
ownership register was obtained.
• What equipment, finishing, shipping and partner capabilities are active today? Which claims and
client examples may be published?
• Which customer/product categories drive revenue and contribution, and which repeat? Are
current buyers already in the suggested audiences?
• Which OnPrintShop version, modules and API/export rights are included? Which processes are
manual despite advertised features?
• What does control on our servers mean: local hardware, client-owned cloud accounts, data
residency, no external AI, or practical portability?
• What is the actual bottleneck: insufficient inquiries, slow quoting, artwork errors, production
constraints or retention?
