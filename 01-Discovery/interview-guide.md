# i2Print Discovery — Daniel's Interview Guide

**Use with:** `discovery-agenda.md` (the timed run-of-show) and `questionnaire.md` (the facts *they* fill in beforehand).
**This file:** the questions only **you** can ask, live. The questionnaire collects facts. This guide uncovers the *why*, the hidden constraints, and the things people don't write down. Don't re-ask anything they already answered; reference it instead ("You wrote that about 10 files a week have problems. Walk me through the last one.").

**Rules for the room**
- Ask about **the last real time** something happened, not "usually." Stories beat opinions.
- Let silence sit for 3 seconds before you fill it.
- When they propose a solution ("we need an app"), ask **"What would that let you do?"** to find the underlying need.
- Write down **exact words** they use for products and problems. Those words become the website copy.
- Don't pitch until the Reaction section.

---

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
- "What's a typical day like here?"
- "What made you decide *now* is the time to fix the website?" *(It surfaces the trigger: a lost customer, a competitor, a bad review, a slow season.)*
- "Have you worked with a web designer or agency before? What went well, and what didn't?" *(Tells you their scar tissue and what to avoid repeating.)*

## 2. Context: how work actually flows (10 min)
**Ask them to show you, not tell you.** If you're at the shop, ask to watch one order come in.
- "Walk me through the last online order, from the moment it arrived to the moment the customer had it."
  - Probe: "Where did it slow down? Who touched it? Did anything need a phone call?"
- "Now the last walk-in or phone order. How's that different?"
- "What share of online orders need someone to step in by hand?"
- "When a file is bad, what exactly happens? Who notices, how do you tell the customer, and how long until it's fixed?"
  - Probe: "Do you ever just fix it yourselves without telling them? Do you charge for it?"
- "Where do new customers come from today: Google, walk-by, referrals, repeat, resellers?" *(Compare with what they wrote on the questionnaire. Gaps between the two are insight.)*

## 3. Deep dive (20 min)

### A. Customers (who the website is really for)
- "Describe your best customer, the one you wish you had 50 more of."
- "Describe the customer you'd rather not have. What makes them hard?"
- "What do customers say to you when they're happy? Exact words if you can remember."
- "What's the most common question people call or email about?" *(These are FAQ items and assistant answers.)*
- "Why do people choose you over Vistaprint or 4OVER4? Why do some leave?"
- "Who places orders at your business customers: the owner, an office manager, a designer?" *(It changes the tone and features: reorders, invoices, team accounts.)*

### B. The file-fixer idea (validate before building)
- "When you imagined the AI file fixer, what problem were you picturing? Tell me about a specific job."
- "If the tool fixed the shape automatically, would you trust it to go straight to print, or would someone still check?"
- "Which products cause the most file trouble?" *(Business cards vs. banners need different rules.)*
- "What would you be uncomfortable with AI changing?" *(Logos, faces, text, brand colors.)*
- "If this saved X hours a week, what would that be worth to you?" *(This anchors the tool's price to value, not to your hours.)*

### C. Online store and OnPrintShop
- "If you could change one thing about how people order online, what would it be?"
- "Do you know where customers give up? Has anyone told you, 'I tried to order online but…'?"
- "Who owns the OnPrintShop account, the domain, and the Google account? Can we get admin access to look around?"
- "Is anything locked in? A contract term, custom pricing rules, integrations with your production software?"
- "Do you use any other software to run orders: a production queue, invoicing, QuickBooks?" *(Integration risk.)*

### C2. Leaving OnPrintShop and the previous developer (confirmed they want to move on)
**Guardrails:**
- Facts only, and no opinions about the previous developer.
- Don't advise on getting out of the contract; their attorney handles that.
- Don't touch any system until access is formally handed over.

**The contract and the exit**
- "Which contract are you in: with the developer, with OnPrintShop, or both? When does it end, and has your attorney looked at it?"
- "Is anything in dispute, or likely to be?" *(If yes, keep your work strictly forward-looking.)*

**What they're leaving and why**
- "What did you hire them to do, and what did you actually get?" *(Listen; don't react.)*
- "What were 'the results you wanted,' in numbers?" *(This becomes your success metric.)*
- "What made you decide to leave OnPrintShop? Cost, missing features, support, or the developer?"
- "What does OnPrintShop do today that you'd miss?" *(Pricing rules? Templates? Proofs? This is the must-keep list.)*

**Data, accounts and ownership**
- "Can you export your products, prices, customers, orders and templates? Have you ever tried?"
- "Who owns the templates and designs the developer built?"
- "Who controls the domain, DNS, hosting and Google accounts right now?"

**Shaping the new system**
- "How complicated is your pricing? Show me how a banner or business-card price is worked out."
- "Who updates products, prices and banners each week? How comfortable are they with software?"
- "When's your slowest season? That's when we'd switch stores."
- "What would make you trust a new developer this time?"

### D. Marketing, email and growth
- "What happened the last time you sent a promo email? Did orders come in?"
- "What times of year are busy or slow?" *(This drives the email calendar.)*
- "Who would write or approve emails and social posts, and how much time can they give each month?" *(Set realistic expectations for self-run vs. managed.)*
- "Are there customer groups you want more of? Restaurants, real estate, schools, events, political campaigns?"

### E. Success, budget and decision (business terms)
- "Six months after launch, what would make you say this was worth it?" Push for a number or an observable change: "more orders" → "how many more?", "fewer calls" → "about what?"
- "How will we know? Do you track orders, revenue by channel, or quote requests today?" *(If not, measuring is part of the scope.)*
- "What's the budget you had in mind, even roughly? Is it one-time, monthly, or both?"
- "Who else has a say? Partner, family, accountant?"
- "Are you talking to anyone else about this project?" *(Ask it plainly and without pressure; it tells you about price sensitivity.)*
- "What would make you say no to this?"

### F. Risk and responsibilities (your risk-manager questions)
- "Do you require vendors to carry insurance or be named as additional insured?" *(Check your COI before you promise.)*
- "Who handles customer data and files today? Any customers with confidentiality requirements, like legal, medical or political work?"
- "Have you ever had a website outage, hack, or payment problem?"
- "Who should I contact if something breaks after hours?"
- "Are you OK with me showing this project in my portfolio once it's live?"

## 4. Reaction (10 min): show the concept
Show the homepage concept and the file-check demo. **Hand them the mouse.**
- "Without me explaining, what do you think this page is for? What would you click first?"
- "What feels like *you*? What doesn't?"
- "Is anything here something you couldn't promise customers?" *(Turnaround, pickup, free file check.)*
- On the demo: "Drop in a file that gave you trouble last week." *(If they have one handy, that's the best possible test.)*
- "What's missing that your customers would expect?"

**Watch for:** hesitation, "we don't really do that," or excitement about a specific piece. Note the exact moment and the words.

## 5. Wrap-up (5 min)
- "What should I have asked that I didn't?"
- "Is there anyone on your team I should talk to for 15 minutes? Whoever handles files or the front counter?"
- "Could I talk to two or three of your customers? Short calls, I'd keep them quick." *(See the research plan below.)*
- Confirm the next step and the date you'll send the Phase One scope.

## Red flags to listen for
| You hear | What it may mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Just make it look modern" | No clear goal | Go back to the success questions; get one measurable outcome |
| "My nephew built the last one" | Low budget anchor, or a relationship in the way | Ask about budget early; show the value math |
| "We need it in two weeks" | Unrealistic timeline | Offer a quick win (banner fixes, Google profile) in week 1–2; phase the rest |
| "Can it do everything Vistaprint does?" | Scope creep | Anchor on Path A plus one standout feature (file check) |
| Nobody knows who owns the domain or OnPrintShop login | Access risk | Make access recovery the first task in Phase 1 |
| "We'll pay when it's done" | Cash-flow risk for you | Propose a deposit plus milestone payments in the agreement |

## Before the call: questions for yourself
Answer these privately so you walk in clear.
1. **Walk-away price.** What's the lowest Phase 1 price you'd accept? What's the full-project budget below which you'd pass?
2. **Capacity.** How many hours a week can you really give this alongside other work? That sets the timeline you promise.
3. **What you won't do.** For example: no logo redesign, no print-production software integration, no 24/7 support. Decide before they ask.
4. **Payment terms you'll propose.** Deposit %, milestones, net terms.
5. **Ownership.** Who owns the code, accounts and content after launch? (Default: they own their accounts and content.) What do you keep the right to reuse, like generic components?
6. **Support boundaries.** What does "monthly care" include, and what costs extra?
7. **Insurance confirmed?** Have you checked the COI wording, E&O scope, and whether cyber coverage is needed?
8. **Portfolio rule.** CCP only. Nothing from prior employment, and no FBM work.
